Opening thesis

Designing a food ordering product that felt calm, premium, and easy to operate under real constraints.

The first frame should work like an executive summary. It states the product problem, the level of ownership, and the kind of craft the user is about to see.

Problem framing

Explain the operational challenge before showing interface surfaces.

Case studies become more credible when the constraints are explicit: menu complexity, customer trust, delivery expectations, and the need for a system that scales without losing clarity.

  • Clarify what was broken or missing before the redesign work started.
  • Name the decisions that changed the product direction or information architecture.
  • Use copy that sounds like product review, not portfolio filler.

Annotated flow or interface surface

Outcome cards can sit beside the narrative, not only at the end.

Desktop case study / Artifact archive

A darker, more editorial layout for process evidence and design reviews.

This alternative is useful for technical or system-heavy projects. It treats the case study like an archive page with stronger contrast and more space for artifacts, diagrams, and decision logs.

Use this mode for enterprise, AI, or systems work where architecture needs more room than marketing visuals.
Group artifacts by decision, not by media type, so the page still feels authored.

Decision chapter

Explain what changed, who it affected, and why the tradeoff mattered.

Readers should be able to skim a case study and still understand the strategic decisions. This layout gives decision logs and proofs equal weight with the visuals.

  • Pair diagrams with plain-language rationale.
  • Call out constraints, dependencies, and implementation consequences.
  • Use the darker surface to create a distinct mode for technical depth.
"For AI and platform work, the reasoning is part of the design. The page should show that."

That makes system-heavy projects feel intentional instead of over-explained.

Motion system

Use small transitions between chapters, not continuous movement.

Fades, slide-ins, and chapter index updates are enough to support reading without distracting from the artifacts.

Content rule

Every artifact needs a job.

If a screenshot cannot support a decision, outcome, or workflow explanation, it does not belong in the page.
